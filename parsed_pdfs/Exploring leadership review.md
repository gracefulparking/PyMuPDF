With the accessible language and user-

appreciate about previous editions, the revised
edition of Exploring Leadership features a
more streamlined and condensed 12-chapter

Leadership Model.
The most significant updates from previous
editions appear in the second part of the text,
which focuses on personal leadership potential.
The authors introduce new frameworks in
the _Understanding Yourself_ chapter, including
mindfulness, emotional intelligence, and
strengths philosophy. Gaining greater aware­
ness on these aspects of self inform modern
leadership challenges. Mindfulness is intro­
duced as “a holistic way of living and being
in tune with yourself and what is happening
around you” (p. 160), which pairs nicely with
the conversation on emotional intelligence.
Strengths philosophy is rooted in positive
psychology and encourages individuals to
focus on developing areas of natural talent into
unique areas of strength rather than dwelling
on weakness (Buckingham & Clifton, 2001).
When applied to leadership development, a
strengths-based approach suggests all can lead
in any context, but engagement depends upon
their unique strengths.

_Reviewed by_ Melissa L. Rocco and

The 2013 edition of _Exploring Leadership_
_for College Students Who Want to Make a_
_Difference_ features new material organized
in a way that helps readers make meaning
of the content and apply it to their own
experiences. The text incorporates modern
notions of leadership as a relational process and
introduces the Relational Leadership Model
(Komives, Lucas, & McMahon, 1998, 2006,
2013), explaining leadership as purposive,
inclusive, empowering, ethical, and process-
oriented. This model serves as a framework
throughout the book, and is expanded upon
with human and organizational development
concepts to help students better understand
themselves, the diversity around them, and
their engagement with their communities.
As with previous editions, the text is
designed for undergraduate and graduate
students engaged in leadership development
experiences on college campuses. The text
includes summary overviews, activities,
reflection questions, and additional reading
suggestions that help students apply concepts,
as well as assist faculty and staff with curriculum
planning and program development. This

The authors have updated the chapter
on _Understanding Others_ to highlight the
importance of diverse perspectives in the
leadership process. Recent research suggesting
the importance of socio-cultural conversations
in developing students’ self-efficacy for
leadership (e.g., Dugan, Kodama, Correia, &
Associates, 2013; Dugan & Komives, 2010)
supports this focus. An added discussion on
inter­cultural sensitivity and microaggressions
helps students understand systemic aspects
of discrimination and the nuance behind
language and behavior when engaging across
difference. These sections are notable and
important revisions that provide a foundation
for the next sections on communication,
conflict resolution, decision-making, empathy,
difficult dialogues, and cultural influences on
leadership behavior.
The authors close part 2 with _Leading_
_with Integrity_. Here, the authors address
learning from responses to ethical dilemmas,
using more modern case studies to illustrate
the increasing complexity of morality in
society. From the child molestation case at
Pennsylvania State University to praised
business models of employee assistance and
sustainability, the chapter highlights both
positive and negative decision making in times
of tested integrity.
Part 3 focuses on context for leadership
and begins with _Being in Communities_. This
chapter explains leadership as a process
and community effort that requires shared
goals, mutual assistance, and valuing diverse
perspectives. Next is _Interacting in Teams_
_and Groups_, which previously served as the
introductory chapter to part 3. The swap is
fitting, as team and group experiences are
ripe for creating and sustaining community.
