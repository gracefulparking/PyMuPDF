# Intellectual Lineage: Leadership Identity Development
*Literature window: 1996–present*

---

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
ANCESTRY: WHAT THIS LITERATURE INHERITED
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

The Leadership Identity Development (LID) model is a hybrid. It inherited from at least four distinct intellectual lineages outside higher education, then fused them inside the student affairs research tradition. Naming the ancestors matters because the seams are visible in the model itself.

ANCESTOR 1 — Post-industrial leadership theory (Joseph Rost, James MacGregor Burns, Margaret Wheatley).
Rost's *Leadership for the Twenty-First Century* (1991) attacked what he called the "industrial paradigm" of leadership: hierarchical, position-bound, great-man centered. Burns (1978) had already split "transactional" from "transformational" leadership. Wheatley brought systems and relational metaphors. Komives and colleagues imported this wholesale through the *Social Change Model* (HERI 1996) and *Exploring Leadership* (Komives, Lucas & McMahon, 1998). What translated cleanly: leadership as relational process rather than positional role. What got lost: Rost's insistence that leadership requires an *intended substantive change* — the SCM and LID kept the relational ethos but softened the change requirement into "common purpose" and personal development.

ANCESTOR 2 — Constructive-developmental psychology (Robert Kegan, William Perry, Jane Loevinger).
Kegan's *In Over Our Heads* (1994) — the very book Harkins reviews in this corpus — gave higher ed the language of "orders of consciousness" and the claim that modern life demands a self-authoring mind. Marcia Baxter Magolda and Patricia King were the primary importers; their self-authorship work became the cognitive scaffolding that LID's later stages (generativity, integration/synthesis) implicitly assume. What translated cleanly: the idea of qualitative shifts in how the self is organized. What was lost: Kegan's stark warning that most adults *don't* reach self-authorship — LID is more optimistic about the developmental arc than Kegan ever was.

ANCESTOR 3 — Psychosocial identity theory (Erikson → Chickering → Chickering & Reisser).
Erikson's identity-vs-role-confusion crisis became, through Chickering's seven vectors, the dominant grammar of college student development. LID inherits the assumption that identity is the developmental task of college and that it proceeds in describable sequences. What translated cleanly: stage logic, the centrality of identity. What got lost or strained: Erikson's psychoanalytic depth, and his insistence that identity is forged against historical and cultural crisis.

ANCESTOR 4 — Grounded theory methodology (Glaser & Strauss 1967; Strauss & Corbin 1990; Charmaz).
The 2005 article is methodologically as much an inheritance as theoretically. Komives et al. used a Strauss-and-Corbin variant of grounded theory to interview 13 students nominated as exhibiting relational leadership. What translated cleanly: the constant comparative method and the production of a stage model. What got lost: grounded theory's original anti-foundationalist edge — the LID model has been treated by subsequent practitioners as a near-universal developmental sequence rather than as a theory grounded in a specific, small, purposively selected sample at a single institution.

FOUNDING WORKS AND THEIR MOVES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ ASTIN 1984: Student Involvement
  MOVE: Reframed college impact as a function of *quantity and quality of student energy invested*, not of what institutions deliver. Shifted the locus from program to person-in-environment.
  RESPONDING TO: Input-output research (his own earlier work included) that treated students as passive recipients of institutional effects.
  ENABLED: Decades of co-curricular justification, including the entire empirical premise that leadership develops through *involvement* — which is exactly the soil LID grows in. Without Astin's involvement theory, there is no warrant for studying leadership through participation in student organizations.

▸ CHICKERING & REISSER 1993/2019: Education and Identity
  MOVE: Operationalized Eriksonian identity into seven trackable vectors college educators could intervene on.
  RESPONDING TO: The absence of a college-specific developmental scheme; Erikson was too broad, Perry too narrowly cognitive.
  ENABLED: Essentially every psychosocial identity model that followed, including Jones & McEwen's MMDI and the LID model itself. LID cites Chickering & Reisser as a direct foundation.

▸ JONES & McEWEN 2000: Multiple Dimensions of Identity
  MOVE: Argued that identity dimensions (race, gender, sexuality, class, religion) orbit a "core" sense of self and are made salient by context — identity is not additive but relational and contextually activated.
  RESPONDING TO: Single-axis identity models (Cross on race, Helms on whiteness, D'Augelli on sexuality) that couldn't speak to each other.
  ENABLED: The intersectional turn in student affairs, and the conceptual space in which one can later ask whether LID's "leader identity" interacts with race, gender, and class identities — though LID itself did not really take up this question.

▸ KING & BAXTER MAGOLDA 1996: A Developmental Perspective on Learning
  MOVE: Argued that learning outcomes and developmental outcomes are the same thing seen from different angles — fused cognition and identity.
  RESPONDING TO: The institutional separation of "academic affairs" (learning) from "student affairs" (development).
  ENABLED: Self-authorship as the integrative construct of the next twenty years, and the cognitive substrate that LID's later stages quietly depend on.

▸ KOMIVES, OWEN, LONGERBEAM, MAINELLA & OSTEEN 2005: Developing a Leadership Identity
  MOVE: Recast leadership from a *skill set or position* to an *identity that develops* — a stage-based self-concept rather than a behavioral repertoire. This is the central pivot. It is also the move that converts leadership education from training to development.
  RESPONDING TO: A leadership education field that, despite the post-industrial rhetoric of the SCM, was still operating with skills-checklist program design. There was no developmental theory specific to leadership.
  ENABLED: The Multi-Institutional Study of Leadership (MSL, 2006–), the leadership minor as a curricular form, an entire NACA/ACPA programming logic, and a generation of dissertations.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTENSIONS: WORKS THAT BUILT ON THE FOUNDATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ KOMIVES ET AL. 2006: A Leadership Identity Development Model: Applications from a Grounded Theory
  EXTENDS: Komives et al. 2005.
  THE EXTENSION: Translated the empirical stages into a usable practitioner model — adding developmental influences (adult mentors, peers, meaningful involvement, reflective learning) and a "developing self" / "group influences" / "changing view of self with others" tripartite scaffolding around the six stages.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The stage logic, the optimistic arc through integration/synthesis, and the implicit assumption that the sampled students' trajectory is generalizable. The 2006 paper does not revisit who was in the original sample or what the sample's racial, class, or institutional composition might have meant for the model's reach.

▸ KOMIVES, LUCAS & McMAHON 2007: Exploring Leadership (2nd ed.)
  EXTENDS: Komives, Lucas & McMahon 1998 and Komives et al. 2005/2006.
  THE EXTENSION: Rebuilt the leading undergraduate leadership textbook around the LID stages and the relational leadership model, making LID curricular rather than only theoretical.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The relational leadership model's assumption that "purposeful, inclusive, empowering, ethical, process-oriented" leadership is the developmental telos. The book does not seriously entertain whether students from minoritized backgrounds may already inhabit collectivist leadership frames that the model treats as a late achievement.

▸ ABES, JONES & McEWEN 2007: Reconceptualizing the MMDI
  EXTENDS: Jones & McEwen 2000.
  THE EXTENSION: Added a "meaning-making filter" drawn explicitly from Baxter Magolda's self-authorship and Kegan's orders of consciousness. The filter explains *why* contextual influences are more or less determinative for different students — formulaic meaning-makers are more shaped by context, self-authoring students more agentic.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The core/dimensions architecture and the assumption that self-authorship represents developmental maturity (a Kegan inheritance that critical scholars would later contest).

▸ BAXTER MAGOLDA 2009: Authoring Your Life
  EXTENDS: Baxter Magolda 2001.
  THE EXTENSION: Pushed self-authorship from a college-years framework into early adulthood, and operationalized the "Learning Partnerships Model" as a pedagogy.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The neo-Kegan developmental telos — self-authorship as the goal — and a largely middle-class professional sample whose pathways get coded as the developmental sequence.

▸ MUSEUS & JAYAKUMAR 2012: Creating Campus Cultures
  EXTENDS: Astin 1984 (involvement), Tinto 1987 (integration), Yosso 2005 (community cultural wealth).
  THE EXTENSION: Argued that campus *cultures*, not just programs, determine racially diverse students' outcomes; shifted the unit of analysis from student behavior to institutional environment.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The basic outcomes vocabulary (persistence, success) of the Astin/Tinto tradition, even while challenging its assumptions about who must do the adjusting.

▸ MUSEUS 2017: CECE Model
  EXTENDS: Museus & Jayakumar 2012.
  THE EXTENSION: Specified nine indicators of culturally engaging campus environments and tied them to sense of belonging and persistence.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: A positivist, model-building orientation that mirrors what Astin and Tinto were doing — using the master's tools on the master's question, which is part of why it has been adoptable by administrators.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
CRITIQUES: WORKS THAT CONTESTED THE FOUNDATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ YOSSO 2005: Whose Culture Has Capital?
  CRITIQUES: Bourdieu's cultural capital as imported into US higher ed research — and, by extension, deficit framings of students of color across the Astin/Tinto/Chickering apparatus.
  FROM WHERE: Critical Race Theory, LatCrit.
  THE CHARGE: That mainstream student development and engagement research treats white middle-class cultural forms as the unmarked norm and students of color as deficient. Communities of color possess aspirational, navigational, social, linguistic, familial, and resistant capital that the dominant frameworks render invisible.
  WAS IT ABSORBED?: Partially, and unevenly. Museus and Jayakumar took it seriously and built CECE/CCW-informed models. The LID literature, by contrast, *cites* Yosso and CRT increasingly in the 2010s but has not fundamentally restructured the six-stage sequence to reflect that students of color may enter college already exercising forms of leadership that the model codes as "leadership differentiated" or even "generativity." The MSL has produced descriptive race-comparative findings but the underlying developmental architecture has not been rebuilt. This is closer to domestication than absorption.

▸ HARKINS 1994 (reviewing KEGAN 1994): In Over Our Heads
  CRITIQUES: Implicitly, any developmental framework that assumes most adults arrive at the highest stages.
  FROM WHERE: Inside the constructive-developmental tradition itself.
  THE CHARGE: Kegan's own data suggest most adults do not reach self-authorship/self-transformation; modernity demands an order of mind most people don't have.
  WAS IT ABSORBED?: In the cognitive-developmental wing (Baxter Magolda, King), yes — partly. In the leadership wing, largely not. LID's "integration/synthesis" stage is presented as achievable by undergraduates in ways Kegan would have found implausible.

Note on incompleteness: this corpus does not include the major critiques of LID that exist in the broader literature — e.g., Dugan's work questioning the universality of the stages, Renn & Bilodeau on LGBTQ leadership identity trajectories, or Beatty and Manning-Ouellette on critical leadership pedagogies. The corpus understates how much critical pushback LID has actually received.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
RECONSTRUCTIONS: WORKS THAT REBUILT AFTER CRITIQUE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ ABES, JONES & McEWEN 2007: Reconceptualized MMDI
  RECONSTRUCTS: Jones & McEwen 2000.
  INCORPORATES: The constructivist critique that the original MMDI was descriptive but not explanatory — it showed identity dimensions in relation to a core but didn't explain *why* contexts mattered differently to different students.
  WHAT SURVIVED: Core identity, contextual influences, intersecting dimensions.
  WHAT WAS ABANDONED: The implicit assumption that contextual salience operates the same way for everyone.
  NEW VOCABULARY: "Meaning-making filter," "formulaic / transitional / foundational" meaning-making.

▸ TORRES, JONES & RENN 2009: Identity Development Theories — Origins, Current Status, New Approaches
  RECONSTRUCTS: The entire psychosocial identity tradition from Chickering forward.
  INCORPORATES: The intersectional and constructivist critiques, and signals (without fully delivering) openness to critical and poststructural approaches.
  WHAT SURVIVED: Identity as the organizing problematic of student affairs theory; developmental framing.
  WHAT WAS ABANDONED: The pretense that any single model could cover identity for all students; the field is reframed as plural.
  NEW VOCABULARY: "Third wave" identity theory; explicit talk of intersectionality as a field-level commitment rather than a niche.

▸ MUSEUS 2017: CECE Model
  RECONSTRUCTS: The Astin/Tinto involvement-and-integration tradition.
  INCORPORATES: Yosso's community cultural wealth, and the broader CRT critique that integration models demand assimilation.
  WHAT SURVIVED: The model-building, measurable-indicators orientation; the persistence/success outcome frame.
  WHAT WAS ABANDONED: The placement of adjustment burden on the student; cultural neutrality of the campus environment.
  NEW VOCABULARY: "Cultural relevance" and "cultural responsiveness" as paired indicators; "culturally engaging environments."

Notably absent from this corpus: a reconstruction of the LID model itself. LID has been extended (2006, 2007 textbook) and applied (MSL) but not fundamentally reconstructed in response to critical critique. That is itself a finding.

LINEAGE CHAINS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

CHAIN 1: The Leadership Identity Line
  Burns (1978) / Rost (1991) — post-industrial leadership theory
    → HERI Social Change Model (1996): import to higher ed; leadership becomes relational and values-laden
    → Komives, Lucas & McMahon 1998 (*Exploring Leadership*): packages SCM as undergraduate pedagogy
    → Komives et al. 2005: the decisive move — leadership becomes a *developmental identity*, not a skill
    → Komives et al. 2006: practitioner translation with developmental influences
    → Komives, Lucas & McMahon 2007: textbook rebuilt around LID
    → MSL (2006–present): turns LID into the largest empirical research program in student leadership
    → Today: LID is the de facto framework of leadership minors, fraternity/sorority educational curricula, and NACA programming. Critical reconstruction has not happened from within the Komives lineage; it is being attempted at the edges (Dugan, Manning-Ouellette, critical leadership pedagogy) but has not displaced the model.

CHAIN 2: The Constructive-Developmental Substrate
  Kegan (1982, 1994) — orders of consciousness
    → Harkins 1994 (review) and King & Baxter Magolda 1996: import into college student learning
    → Baxter Magolda 2001 (*Making Their Own Way*): self-authorship as the integrative construct
    → Abes, Jones & McEwen 2007: self-authorship becomes the meaning-making filter inside the MMDI
    → Baxter Magolda 2009: extension into early adulthood
    → Quietly inside LID: the late stages (generativity, integration/synthesis) presuppose self-authorship cognition without saying so
    → Today: self-authorship remains the dominant cognitive-developmental frame; CRT and poststructural critiques have not displaced it but have made its universality claims defensible only with qualification.

CHAIN 3: The Identity Pluralization Line
  Erikson → Chickering 1969 → Chickering & Reisser 1993/2019
    → Jones & McEwen 2000: pluralizes identity into multiple dimensions
    → Abes, Jones & McEwen 2007: adds cognitive substrate
    → Torres, Jones & Renn 2009: field-level synthesis declaring third-wave identity theory
    → Yosso 2005 (running alongside): pushes from outside via CRT
    → Today: the dominant frame in student affairs identity research; intersectionality is rhetorically central, though critics argue intersectionality has been domesticated into a variable rather than retained as a structural critique.

CHAIN 4: The Environments Line (a parallel chain LID does not really engage)
  Astin 1984 (involvement) and Tinto 1987 (integration)
    → Yosso 2005: CRT critique of the cultural neutrality assumption
    → Museus & Jayakumar 2012: shifts unit of analysis to campus culture
    → Museus 2017 (CECE): formal model
    → Today: CECE has become the leading framework for campus environment research with minoritized students. Notably, LID and CECE have not been brought into serious conversation — leadership identity research still tends to treat the campus environment as a context for individual development rather than as a racialized cultural system.

TENSIONS THAT REMAIN LIVE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

TENSION 1: Is leadership identity universal or culturally specific?
  The disagreement: LID presents a six-stage developmental sequence as broadly generalizable; critical and CRT-informed scholars argue that students from collectivist, communalist, or activist traditions may enter college already exercising "leadership differentiated" or "generativity" practices, making the early stages either irrelevant or insulting.
  Positions: Komives and the MSL camp continue to find empirical support for the stages across populations; critical leadership scholars (Dugan in his later work, Beatty, Manning-Ouellette, Pendakur) argue the model's developmental telos encodes white middle-class organizational norms.
  Why unresolved: The MSL's massive sample produces aggregate confirmation, while the critique operates at the level of meaning and is harder to falsify with survey data. The two camps are using incommensurate evidence.

TENSION 2: Self-authorship as universal developmental goal vs. cultural imposition.
  The disagreement: Is the move toward an internally defined, self-authored voice a universal developmental achievement, or a culturally specific (Western, individualist) ideal smuggled in as development?
  Positions: Baxter Magolda and the Miami/Maryland lineage maintain self-authorship's universality. Vasti Torres has pushed back, showing that for Latino students self-authorship is interwoven with familial and community authority in ways that the Kegan-derived model handles awkwardly. Critical and indigenous scholars go further and reject the individuated self as the telos at all.
  Why unresolved: Self-authorship has been operationally so productive — it organizes assessment, curriculum, and research — that abandoning it would require rebuilding much of the field's infrastructure.
