# Intellectual Lineage: Multiple Dimensions of Identity
*Literature window: 2000–present*

---

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
ANCESTRY: WHAT THIS LITERATURE INHERITED
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

The Multiple Dimensions of Identity (MMDI) model sits at the confluence of four older streams, none of which originated in higher education. Naming them honestly matters because the field has a habit of citing ancestors without acknowledging what was changed in the importing.

• Erik Erikson's psychosocial identity theory (1950s–60s). Erikson argued identity formed in stages through resolution of psychosocial crises, with adolescence as the pivotal moment for identity-versus-role-confusion. Chickering imported this directly into higher education in 1969 (Education and Identity), and Chickering & Reisser's 1993 revision remained Erikson-inflected. What translated cleanly: the developmental, stage-like architecture and the assumption that college is identity's crucible. What was lost: Erikson's clinical depth and his attention to historical/political context. The college student affairs version flattened Erikson into vectors.

• Nigrescence and racial identity models (Cross 1971/1991; Helms 1990). These were Black psychology's response to the Eriksonian mainstream — arguing that racial identity had its own developmental logic that white-normed models could not see. Jones & McEwen (2000) cite both directly. What translated: the idea that minoritized identities develop along distinct paths. What was lost: the political stakes. Cross's original "Negro-to-Black conversion experience" was explicitly tied to Black Power; by the time it entered student affairs handbooks, it had become a typology.

• Robert Kegan's constructive-developmental psychology (1982, 1994). Kegan's "orders of consciousness" describe how meaning-making capacity itself develops — what we can hold as object versus what holds us as subject. Imported into higher education by Marcia Baxter Magolda and Patricia King, who built self-authorship theory on Kegan's third-to-fourth order transition. The 2007 Abes/Jones/McEwen reconceptualization is the moment Kegan enters MMDI explicitly. What translated: the elegant idea that development is about complexity of meaning-making, not content. What was lost: Kegan's clinical precision. In student affairs, "self-authorship" became something close to a learning outcome.

• Kimberlé Crenshaw's intersectionality (1989, 1991). This is the most contested import. Crenshaw was a critical legal scholar writing about how Black women fell through the cracks of antidiscrimination law because courts treated race and sex as separate axes. Intersectionality was a structural and legal argument about power, not a developmental description of how individuals experience identity. Jones & McEwen (2000) and the 2007 reconceptualization both invoke Crenshaw. What translated: the multi-axis insight, the rejection of single-identity analysis. What was lost — and this is the field's most consequential mistranslation — the political economy of intersectionality. The MMDI renders intersectionality as a phenomenology of salience ("which identity feels most present right now?") rather than as an analysis of how interlocking systems of oppression produce particular subjects. Crenshaw herself has objected to exactly this kind of psychologization.

FOUNDING WORKS AND THEIR MOVES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ CHICKERING & REISSER 1993/2019: Education and Identity
  MOVE: Codified college student development as a set of seven vectors (competence, emotions, autonomy, identity, etc.), giving the field a shared vocabulary for what college was supposed to do to students psychologically.
  RESPONDING TO: An absence of higher-ed-specific developmental theory; Erikson had to be translated for an institutional context.
  ENABLED: Virtually every subsequent identity model in student affairs, including MMDI, defines itself in relation to (or against) the vectors.

▸ ASTIN 1984: Student Involvement
  MOVE: Reframed development as a function of time and energy invested, shifting attention from intrapsychic stages to behavior and environment.
  RESPONDING TO: The psychological turn's neglect of what students actually do on campus.
  ENABLED: A behavioral/environmental tradition (engagement, Tinto, eventually Museus) that runs parallel to the identity tradition and occasionally intersects.

▸ HARKINS 1994: Review of Kegan's In Over Our Heads
  MOVE: A connective tissue piece — brought Kegan's 1994 framework to a student development audience.
  RESPONDING TO: The gap between Kegan's clinical/adult-development register and student affairs.
  ENABLED: Baxter Magolda and King's importation of Kegan, and ultimately the 2007 MMDI reconceptualization that put meaning-making at the center.

▸ JONES & McEWEN 1996/2000: A Conceptual Model of Multiple Dimensions of Identity
  MOVE: Replaced the single-axis identity-stage model with a visual of a core self surrounded by intersecting dimensions, whose salience shifts by context. (The 1996 date in the corpus metadata appears to predate the 2000 JCSD publication that is the canonical citation; treating these as the same intervention.)
  RESPONDING TO: The fact that race models, gender models, and sexual identity models were proliferating in parallel with no theoretical account of how a person held them simultaneously.
  ENABLED: The intersectional turn in student affairs theory; the 2007 reconceptualization; Torres/Jones/Renn's 2009 synthesis; and a generation of qualitative dissertations on identity salience.

▸ KING & BAXTER MAGOLDA 1996: A Developmental Perspective on Learning
  MOVE: Argued learning and identity are the same developmental problem viewed from two angles — both about epistemological complexity.
  RESPONDING TO: The artificial cognitive/psychosocial split inherited from Perry and Chickering.
  ENABLED: Baxter Magolda's self-authorship program, which becomes the meaning-making engine inside the 2007 MMDI.

▸ BAXTER MAGOLDA 2001: Making Their Own Way
  MOVE: Operationalized Kegan's fourth order as "self-authorship" through a longitudinal qualitative study, giving student affairs an empirically grounded developmental target.
  RESPONDING TO: Kegan's abstractness and the field's lack of empirical traction on meaning-making.
  ENABLED: The "meaning-making filter" that Abes/Jones/McEwen 2007 inserts into the MMDI.

▸ YOSSO 2005: Whose Culture Has Capital?
  MOVE: Inverted deficit framings of Students of Color by enumerating six forms of "community cultural wealth" (aspirational, navigational, social, linguistic, familial, resistant).
  RESPONDING TO: Bourdieu's cultural capital as applied to education, which read minoritized students as lacking.
  ENABLED: Museus's CECE work and a CRT-informed wing of the field that often runs alongside, but not into, the MMDI tradition.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTENSIONS: WORKS THAT BUILT ON THE FOUNDATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ ABES, JONES & McEWEN 2007: Reconceptualizing the MMDI
  EXTENDS: Jones & McEwen 2000; Baxter Magolda 2001; Kegan 1994.
  THE EXTENSION: Inserted a "meaning-making filter" between contextual influences and identity perception. At earlier orders of consciousness, contextual messages pass through more or less unfiltered; at later orders, students can hold context as object and choose how to integrate it. This explained variance the 2000 model couldn't: why two students with similar identity configurations experienced them so differently.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The core/dimensions imagery; the psychological register; and — critically — the depoliticized translation of intersectionality. Crenshaw's structural argument was still being rendered as individual phenomenology, just now with a developmental modifier.

▸ KOMIVES et al. 2006: Leadership Identity Development Model
  EXTENDS: Komives et al. 2005 grounded theory; Chickering & Reisser.
  THE EXTENSION: A six-stage model of how students come to see themselves as leaders, treating leadership as an identity rather than a skill set.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: A stage-sequential logic that the MMDI tradition had moved away from; the assumption that "leader" is a generalizable identity rather than one shaped by who is allowed to claim it.

▸ KOMIVES, LUCAS & McMAHON 2007: Exploring Leadership
  EXTENDS: The LID model into a textbook for undergraduate use.
  THE EXTENSION: Pedagogical translation — turning a research model into curriculum.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The relational leadership framework's assumption that leadership identity can be developed somewhat independently of structural position.

▸ BAXTER MAGOLDA 2009: Authoring Your Life
  EXTENDS: Baxter Magolda 2001.
  THE EXTENSION: Pushed self-authorship past college into adult life and introduced "learning partnerships" as the pedagogical mechanism.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The Kegan-derived assumption that internal voice is the developmental telos — a position that critical scholars have flagged as culturally specific (privileging an individuated Western subject).

▸ MUSEUS & JAYAKUMAR 2012: Creating Campus Cultures
  EXTENDS: Yosso 2005; Tinto; Astin.
  THE EXTENSION: Moved CRT-informed analysis from the individual student onto the campus environment, arguing institutions — not students — needed to change.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: A retention-and-success outcomes frame inherited from the Tinto/Astin tradition, which sits uneasily with the more radical implications of Yosso's CRT.

▸ MUSEUS 2017: CECE Model
  EXTENDS: Museus & Jayakumar 2012.
  THE EXTENSION: Specified nine indicators of culturally engaging campus environments and linked them empirically to sense of belonging and persistence.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The persistence/belonging outcomes lens — useful for institutional uptake, but it tames CRT's structural critique into something assessable.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
CRITIQUES: WORKS THAT CONTESTED THE FOUNDATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

The corpus as supplied is thin on direct critiques of the MMDI — most of the sharpest critiques (Stewart, Patton, Jones's own later self-critique in Abes/Jones/Stewart 2019) sit just outside the 16 texts. What's in the corpus is more like adjacent critique, traditions that move past MMDI without engaging it head-on.

▸ YOSSO 2005: Community Cultural Wealth
  CRITIQUES: Implicitly, the entire psychosocial tradition (including MMDI) that locates identity development in the individual student.
  FROM WHERE: Critical Race Theory; LatCrit.
  THE CHARGE: Treating minoritized students through developmental-deficit or even neutral-developmental lenses misses that they arrive with assets the institution fails to recognize. The problem is institutional epistemology, not student development.
  WAS IT ABSORBED?: Partially and unevenly. The MMDI tradition cites Yosso but rarely restructures around her. The CRT wing (Museus, Patton) absorbed it more fully. The two wings coexist more than they converse.

▸ MUSEUS & JAYAKUMAR 2012 / MUSEUS 2017: Campus Environments
  CRITIQUES: The student-development tradition's tendency to treat the student as the unit of intervention.
  FROM WHERE: Cultural and ecological frameworks informed by CRT.
  THE CHARGE: Identity development models, including MMDI, place the analytic burden on the student to navigate identity, while leaving the racialized environment unmarked.
  WAS IT ABSORBED?: Domesticated. The field added "environmental context" to its models — the MMDI already had "contextual influences" as an outer ring — without changing the locus of analysis. The student remains the figure; the environment remains ground.

The most consequential critique of MMDI — that it psychologizes Crenshaw — comes from outside this corpus (Patton et al. 2016; Harris & Patton 2019; Abes/Jones/Stewart 2019). The corpus contains the lineage but not the reckoning.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
RECONSTRUCTIONS: WORKS THAT REBUILT AFTER CRITIQUE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ ABES, JONES & McEWEN 2007: Reconceptualized MMDI
  RECONSTRUCTS: Jones & McEwen 2000.
  INCORPORATES: The Kegan/Baxter Magolda critique that the 2000 model could not explain developmental variation in how identity was experienced.
  WHAT SURVIVED: Core self, intersecting dimensions, salience-by-context, the visual grammar.
  WHAT WAS ABANDONED: The implication that contextual influences acted directly on identity perception. Now they pass through a meaning-making filter.
  NEW VOCABULARY: "Meaning-making filter," "meaning-making capacity," explicit invocation of orders of consciousness.
  WHAT IT DID NOT RECONSTRUCT: The depoliticization of intersectionality. Crenshaw is still cited; structural power analysis is still absent. That reconstruction would have to wait for the third iteration (the I-MMDI, Abes/Jones/Stewart 2019), which lies outside this corpus.

▸ TORRES, JONES & RENN 2009: Identity Development Theories in Student Affairs
  RECONSTRUCTS: The whole identity development sub-field rather than any single model.
  INCORPORATES: The recognition that single-axis models (racial identity, sexual identity, gender identity) and integrative models (MMDI) had developed in parallel without dialogue, and that constructivist and critical traditions were diverging.
  WHAT SURVIVED: The legitimacy of psychosocial identity development as a project.
  WHAT WAS ABANDONED: The pretense that the field had a single paradigm. The essay maps a pluralism and concedes the critical/poststructural turn cannot be reduced to the constructivist one.
  NEW VOCABULARY: Less new vocabulary than new cartography — naming "foundational," "integrative," and "critical/poststructural" as distinct waves.

LINEAGE CHAINS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

CHAIN 1: The MMDI spine
  Erikson (psychosocial identity)
    → Chickering 1969 / Chickering & Reisser 1993: imported as developmental vectors for college
    → Cross 1991 / Helms 1990 (parallel track): racial identity as its own developmental logic
    → Jones & McEwen 2000: collapsed the parallel tracks into a single multi-dimensional model with a salient/non-salient distinction
    → Abes, Jones & McEwen 2007: inserted Kegan-via-Baxter Magolda's meaning-making filter
    → Torres, Jones & Renn 2009: meta-mapped the resulting pluralism
    → Today: the I-MMDI (2019, outside this corpus) tries to put structural power back in; whether it succeeds is contested.

CHAIN 2: The Kegan import
  Kegan 1982, 1994 (constructive-developmental psychology)
    → Harkins 1994: bridge review for the student development audience
    → King & Baxter Magolda 1996: argued learning and identity share an epistemological core
    → Baxter Magolda 2001: operationalized self-authorship empirically through longitudinal narrative
    → Abes, Jones & McEwen 2007: borrowed self-authorship as the meaning-making engine for MMDI
    → Baxter Magolda 2009: extended self-authorship into post-college adult life
    → Today: self-authorship is the field's most-used developmental construct, and its individualist assumptions remain under-examined.

CHAIN 3: The Crenshaw translation (and its discontents)
  Crenshaw 1989, 1991 (intersectionality as structural/legal analysis)
    → Jones & McEwen 2000: imported as a phenomenology of identity salience; structural power dropped
    → Abes, Jones & McEwen 2007: added developmental sophistication without restoring the structural argument
    → Yosso 2005 (parallel track): kept the structural/CRT register but on a different problem (capital, not identity)
    → Critique (largely outside this corpus, Patton, Harris, Stewart): MMDI domesticated intersectionality
    → Today: the field has two intersectionalities running simultaneously — a psychological-developmental one and a CRT/structural one — that cite each other politely without merging.

CHAIN 4: The environment counter-tradition
  Astin 1984 (involvement) and Tinto (integration, outside corpus)
    → Yosso 2005: rejected the assimilation logic with community cultural wealth
    → Museus & Jayakumar 2012: shifted the unit of analysis from student to campus
    → Museus 2017 (CECE): specified the environmental indicators and linked them to belonging
    → Today: the environment tradition has effectively become the field's preferred way to operationalize CRT for institutional consumption — at the cost, critics argue, of CRT's sharper edges.

SCHOLARLY RELATIONSHIPS AND CONVERSATIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

What can be verified from the public record:

• Susan Jones and Marylu McEwen co-authored the 1996/2000 MMDI; both were at the University of Maryland at that time, where McEwen was a long-tenured faculty member in the Counseling and Personnel Services program. Jones later moved to Ohio State, where she has been a central figure in the College Student Personnel program. Ohio State (with Jones, Abes connections, and others) became one of the institutional concentrations for MMDI-tradition work.

• Elisa Abes completed her doctorate at Ohio State and has continued co-authoring with Jones across MMDI iterations (2007, and later with Stewart in 2019). This is one of the field's more visible advisor-collaborator lineages.

• Marcia Baxter Magolda and Patricia King co-authored the 1996 piece in this corpus and have collaborated repeatedly on self-authorship; Baxter Magolda is associated with Miami University (Ohio), King with Michigan. The Miami–Michigan axis is the institutional home of self-authorship research.

• Susan Komives was a long-tenured faculty member at the University of Maryland and is the central figure in the leadership identity development cluster; the recurring co-author group (Owen, Longerbeam, Mainella, Osteen) reflects a Maryland-based research team.

• Vasti Torres, Susan Jones, and Kristen Renn co-authored the 2009 synthesis; this trio represents three institutional traditions (Penn State/Indiana, Ohio State, Michigan State) coming together for the field's stock-taking moment.

• Tara Yosso's work emerged from the UCLA CRT-in-education cluster around Daniel Solórzano, though the corpus does not include the Solórzano texts that ground that lineage.

I am not going to assert specific dissertation-advisor relationships I cannot verify here.

TENSIONS THAT REMAIN LIVE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

• Is intersectionality a developmental construct or a structural one? The MMDI tradition treats it as the former; CRT scholars insist it is the latter and that the translation distorts Crenshaw. The disagreement is unresolved because the two camps publish in adjacent venues, cite each other respectfully, and rarely force the confrontation. The 2019 I-MMDI attempts a reconciliation; many CRT scholars consider it insufficient.

• Is self-authorship culturally universal or a Western individualist ideal? Baxter Magolda's program treats internal voice as the developmental target; critics (Indigenous, Asian American, communalist scholars) argue this privileges autonomy over relationality. The tradition has responded by adding cultural nuance to interview studies without revising the core developmental telos.

• Is the student or the institution the proper unit of intervention? Identity development theories center the student; environmental/CRT models (Museus, Yosso) center the institution. In practice, student affairs continues to operate primarily on students, because that is what the profession is structured to do, regardless of which theory is cited.

• Does "context" mean the same thing across traditions? In MMDI, context is an outer ring of influence on identity salience. In CRT, context is racialized structure that produces subjects. The same word covers very different ontologies, and the field has not forced a reckoning because the ambiguity is professionally useful.

• Is the field's pluralism intellectual maturity or theoretical avoidance? Torres, Jones & Renn 2009 framed the proliferation of identity theories as a productive multi-paradigm moment. Skeptics — including some of the same authors in later work — wonder whether pluralism has become an alibi for not choosing between incompatible commitments. This is the meta-tension under all the others, and the one a doctoral student should learn to see first.
