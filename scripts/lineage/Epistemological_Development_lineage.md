# Intellectual Lineage: Epistemological Development
*Literature window: 1970–present*

---

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
ANCESTRY: WHAT THIS LITERATURE INHERITED
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

Epistemological development as a research program in higher education did not invent itself from scratch. It pulled from three older streams.

JEAN PIAGET (genetic epistemology, 1920s–1970s). Piaget argued that knowing was not a content to be transmitted but a structure that developed through stages — sensorimotor, preoperational, concrete, formal operational. The structural claim (that how a person thinks reorganizes qualitatively, not just quantitatively) is the deep grammar of every model in this corpus. William Perry imported the structural logic directly; he wanted to know whether something analogous to formal operations kept developing in late adolescence and adulthood. What translated cleanly: the idea of qualitative positions, the idea that earlier structures are incorporated rather than discarded, the methodological commitment to interview-based reconstruction of underlying logic. What got lost: Piaget's biological framing and his interest in universal cognitive operations. Perry and his successors localized the structures to meaning-making about knowledge and authority, not logic per se.

LAWRENCE KOHLBERG (moral development, 1958–1984). Kohlberg's six-stage scheme of moral reasoning, also Piagetian in form, was the proximate model Perry was working alongside at Harvard. Kohlberg gave Perry permission to extend Piaget into post-formal territory and to use semi-clinical interviews as the evidentiary base. What translated: the longitudinal interview method, the structural stage logic, the conviction that "higher" stages were genuinely more adequate. What was lost — and this matters for the Belenky critique — was that Kohlberg's male-only sample produced a hierarchy that Carol Gilligan would later argue mis-scored women's reasoning. Perry inherited the same vulnerability; his sample was Harvard men.

JOHN DEWEY (reflective thinking, 1910/1933). Dewey's *How We Think* defined reflective judgment as the suspension of belief while evidence is weighed under conditions of genuine uncertainty. King and Kitchener took this almost word-for-word as the philosophical anchor of the Reflective Judgment Model. What translated: the centrality of ill-structured problems, the idea that mature thinking tolerates uncertainty without collapsing into either dogmatism or relativism. What was lost: Dewey's social and political commitments around inquiry as democratic practice. The cognitive-developmental tradition tends to render reflective judgment as an individual capacity rather than a social one.

CAROL GILLIGAN (*In a Different Voice*, 1982). Not strictly an ancestor — contemporary — but the move Gilligan made on Kohlberg is the move Belenky et al. made on Perry. Gilligan's claim that women's moral reasoning was organized around care and relationship rather than abstract justice gave Belenky, Clinchy, Goldberger and Tarule both a method and a warrant.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
FOUNDING WORKS AND THEIR MOVES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

The corpus you've given me is thin on the epistemological development founders themselves — Perry, Belenky et al., King & Kitchener are not corpus entries, only ancestors. The corpus instead captures the *downstream* foundational works in the constructivist-developmental wing of student affairs. I'll work with what's actually here.

▸ CHICKERING & REISSER 2019 (orig. Chickering 1969): *Education and Identity*
  MOVE: Established the psychosocial stage framework for college student development — seven vectors covering identity, competence, autonomy, purpose. Made the claim that college is a developmental environment, not just a credentialing one.
  RESPONDING TO: A field of student personnel work that lacked a theoretical spine; an Eriksonian framework that stopped short of college-aged specifics.
  ENABLED: Virtually everything in the psychosocial tradition. It is the work that made "student development" a phrase with content. The 2019 second edition with Reisser updated for gender and contextual variation but kept the vector structure intact.

▸ ASTIN 1984: *Student Involvement*
  MOVE: Pivoted from intrapsychic development to behavioral and environmental engagement. Argued that learning outcomes track quantity and quality of time-on-task in the college environment.
  RESPONDING TO: The vagueness of "impact" claims in higher education research. Astin wanted measurable inputs and outputs.
  ENABLED: The engagement literature (Kuh, NSSE) and a generation of institutional research. Less directly relevant to epistemological development, but it set up the empirical-environmental counterpoint to the cognitive-structural tradition.

▸ JONES & McEWEN 1996: *Multiple Dimensions of Identity*
  MOVE: Replaced singular identity models (race-only, gender-only) with a model where multiple identity dimensions orbit a core, made salient by context. Brought intersectional thinking into student affairs theory in a form practitioners could use.
  RESPONDING TO: The proliferation of single-identity models (Cross on Black identity, Helms on White identity, Josselson on women) that could not speak to students who lived multiple identities simultaneously.
  ENABLED: Abes, Jones & McEwen's 2007 reconceptualization; the broader intersectional turn in student affairs scholarship.

▸ KING & BAXTER MAGOLDA 1996: *A Developmental Perspective on Learning*
  MOVE: Welded Perry's epistemological scheme and Kegan's orders of consciousness to a theory of college learning outcomes. Argued that the *point* of college, developmentally, is the move toward self-authored knowing.
  RESPONDING TO: A learning literature dominated by cognitive psychology of skill acquisition that ignored the developmental substrate. Also responding to the gap between Perry's epistemological scheme and what student affairs practitioners actually did.
  ENABLED: Baxter Magolda's *Making Their Own Way* and the entire self-authorship research program.

▸ BAXTER MAGOLDA 2001: *Making Their Own Way*
  MOVE: Reframed Perry/Kegan-style development around a single integrative construct — self-authorship — defined across epistemological, intrapersonal, and interpersonal dimensions. Backed it with a longitudinal interview study (the Wabash-Provost study, running from 1986) that extended past college into the late 20s and 30s.
  RESPONDING TO: The fragmentation of cognitive, identity, and relational development into separate models. Also responding to the underappreciation of epistemological development by practitioners — Baxter Magolda wanted a construct usable in classrooms and residence halls.
  ENABLED: The Learning Partnerships Model, Abes et al.'s 2007 reconceptualization of MMDI through a self-authorship meaning-making filter, and a generation of dissertations operationalizing self-authorship.

▸ YOSSO 2005: *Whose Culture Has Capital?*
  MOVE: Inverted the deficit framing of students of color by enumerating six forms of community cultural wealth (aspirational, navigational, social, linguistic, familial, resistant). Turned Bourdieu's cultural capital on its head from a CRT position.
  RESPONDING TO: Higher education's habit of treating students of color as bringing deficits to be remediated, and to Bourdieu's framing which (Yosso argued) treated dominant capital as the only capital.
  ENABLED: Museus & Jayakumar's campus culture work; the asset-based turn in retention scholarship. Not part of the epistemological development lineage proper, but a sibling foundational move in the same era's student development theorizing.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
EXTENSIONS: WORKS THAT BUILT ON THE FOUNDATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ HARKINS 1994: Review of Kegan's *In Over Our Heads*
  EXTENDS: Kegan 1994 — not an original extension but a marker that Kegan's orders of consciousness framework was being absorbed into the constructivist-developmental conversation.
  THE EXTENSION: Surfaces Kegan's argument that modern life makes cognitive-developmental demands most adults cannot meet. This is the framing Baxter Magolda would build on.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The hierarchical-stage assumption — that later orders are more adequate to modern demands. This assumption rarely gets questioned in the corpus.

▸ ABES, JONES & McEWEN 2007: Reconceptualizing the MMDI
  EXTENDS: Jones & McEwen 1996.
  THE EXTENSION: Added a "meaning-making filter" between context and identity perception, drawing on Baxter Magolda's self-authorship. Students with more complex meaning-making could resist contextual pressures on identity; students with formulaic meaning-making absorbed them.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The core-and-dimensions architecture. Also the assumption that more complex meaning-making is developmentally preferable — an assumption inherited from the constructivist-developmental tradition that critical scholars would later challenge.

▸ BAXTER MAGOLDA 2009: *Authoring Your Life*
  EXTENDS: Baxter Magolda 2001.
  THE EXTENSION: Pushed the longitudinal data later into adulthood; refined the internal voice construct; developed the Learning Partnerships Model as the pedagogical companion to the developmental theory.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The teleology — self-authorship remains the destination. The critique that this telos is itself culturally specific (individualist, Western, middle-class) is acknowledged in passing but not structurally absorbed.

▸ KOMIVES ET AL. 2006: Leadership Identity Development Model
  EXTENDS: Komives et al. 2005 grounded theory; draws on Chickering & Reisser and Kegan.
  THE EXTENSION: Six-stage developmental model of how college students come to understand themselves as leaders, moving from leader-as-position to leadership-as-process.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: Stage logic and the assumption of a universal trajectory. Sample diversity was better than Perry's but the stages are still presented as generalizable.

▸ KOMIVES, LUCAS & McMAHON 2007: *Exploring Leadership*
  EXTENDS: The LID research program into a textbook.
  THE EXTENSION: Packaged the developmental theory for undergraduate use; the practitioner translation move.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: All the developmental assumptions, now flattened into curriculum.

▸ MUSEUS & JAYAKUMAR 2012: *Creating Campus Cultures*
  EXTENDS: Yosso 2005, Tinto, Astin.
  THE EXTENSION: Moved from individual student assets to campus cultural conditions that activate or suppress them.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: An institutional-effects framing inherited from Astin/Tinto, even as the cultural content is reworked.

▸ MUSEUS 2017: CECE Model
  EXTENDS: Museus & Jayakumar 2012.
  THE EXTENSION: A nine-indicator model of culturally engaging campus environments tied to belonging and persistence outcomes.
  WHAT STAYED THE SAME: The outcomes-and-environments structure of engagement research — Astin's grammar with critical content poured in.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
CRITIQUES: WORKS THAT CONTESTED THE FOUNDATION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

The corpus is light on direct critiques of epistemological development. The most consequential historical critique — Belenky, Clinchy, Goldberger & Tarule's *Women's Ways of Knowing* (1986) — is not in the corpus but is foundational context, so I'll treat it here.

▸ BELENKY, CLINCHY, GOLDBERGER & TARULE 1986: *Women's Ways of Knowing* (corpus-adjacent)
  CRITIQUES: Perry 1970.
  FROM WHERE: Feminist epistemology, in the Gilligan lineage.
  THE CHARGE: Perry's nine-position scheme, built on Harvard men, mistook a male trajectory for a universal one. Specifically, it had no place for *silenced* knowing (the position of those whose voices have been suppressed) or *connected* knowing (knowing through identification and empathy rather than detached critique). What Perry called the highest position — committed relativism — privileged a particular separate-knowing style.
  WAS IT ABSORBED?: Partially, and unevenly. The vocabulary was absorbed quickly — every textbook now includes WWK alongside Perry. The methodological lesson (sample matters, gender is not noise) was absorbed. But the structural critique — that the developmental hierarchy itself encodes a gendered epistemic preference — was largely domesticated. Baxter Magolda's early work explicitly built in gender-related patterns but, by the 2001 self-authorship synthesis, gendered patterns had been reabsorbed into a single developmental trajectory. The critique that there are *different ways* of knowing, not better and worse positions on one ladder, did not survive the integration.

▸ YOSSO 2005: *Whose Culture Has Capital?* (functions as a parallel critique)
  CRITIQUES: Deficit-oriented student development and retention scholarship; implicitly the constructivist-developmental tradition's assumption that some meaning-making is more adequate than others.
  FROM WHERE: Critical Race Theory.
  THE CHARGE: The dominant frameworks measure students of color against a white middle-class developmental yardstick and call the gap "deficit."
  WAS IT ABSORBED?: Citationally, heavily. Substantively, partially. The asset-based language entered the field but the developmental hierarchies in self-authorship and identity work largely continued. Abes' later work tries to bridge this; most of the field cites Yosso ritually.

The corpus does not contain a frontal CRT or queer critique of the epistemological development tradition specifically. This is itself a finding: epistemological development has been relatively insulated from the critical turn that reshaped identity development. Practitioners' underappreciation may be partial protection.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
RECONSTRUCTIONS: WORKS THAT REBUILT AFTER CRITIQUE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

▸ BAXTER MAGOLDA 2001: *Making Their Own Way*
  RECONSTRUCTS: Perry's scheme and Kegan's orders, post-WWK.
  INCORPORATES: The methodological lesson from Belenky et al. — Baxter Magolda's longitudinal sample was gender-balanced from the start (1986 cohort), and she initially reported gender-related epistemological patterns.
  WHAT SURVIVED: The developmental trajectory; the assumption that more complex meaning-making is the goal; the interview methodology.
  WHAT WAS ABANDONED: Perry's nine positions collapsed into a smaller number of phases; explicit gender-pattern reporting receded in favor of a unified self-authorship construct.
  NEW VOCABULARY: Self-authorship; external formulas; crossroads; internal foundation; the three dimensions (epistemological, intrapersonal, interpersonal).

▸ ABES, JONES & McEWEN 2007: Reconceptualized MMDI
  RECONSTRUCTS: Jones & McEwen 1996, post-self-authorship.
  INCORPORATES: The constructivist-developmental insight that how a student processes context matters as much as the context itself.
  WHAT SURVIVED: The multiple-dimensions architecture; the core-identity construct.
  WHAT WAS ABANDONED: The implicit assumption that contextual influences operate uniformly across students.
  NEW VOCABULARY: Meaning-making filter; formulaic vs. foundational meaning-making in identity terms.

▸ TORRES, JONES & RENN 2009: Identity Development Theories in Student Affairs
  RECONSTRUCTS: The whole identity development field for the *Journal of College Student Development*'s 50th anniversary.
  INCORPORATES: Intersectional and constructivist critiques; the move toward integrative models.
  WHAT SURVIVED: The category of "identity development" itself as the organizing principle; the assumption that developmental theory is the field's spine.
  WHAT WAS ABANDONED: The pretense that single-identity stage models are sufficient.
  NEW VOCABULARY: Less new vocabulary than new organization — the article's contribution is a periodization (origins, current status, future directions) that has shaped how the field tells its own history.

LINEAGE CHAINS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

CHAIN 1: The cognitive-structural through-line (the central chain for this concept)
  Piaget (genetic epistemology) + Kohlberg (moral stages)
    → Perry 1970 [not in corpus]: imported structural-stage logic into college epistemology; nine positions from dualism to committed relativism, on a Harvard male sample.
    → Belenky et al. 1986 [not in corpus]: critiqued Perry's sample and surfaced silenced and connected knowing; raised the question whether the hierarchy itself was gendered.
    → King & Kitchener (Reflective Judgment, 1970s–1994) [not in corpus]: extended Perry's framework with a tighter Deweyan philosophical anchor and longitudinal data into adulthood.
    → King & Baxter Magolda 1996: welded Perry, Kegan, and reflective judgment into a theory of college learning.
    → Baxter Magolda 2001: reconstructed the tradition around self-authorship; absorbed methodological lessons from WWK but largely domesticated the substantive critique.
    → Baxter Magolda 2009: pushed the construct later into adulthood, developed the pedagogical Learning Partnerships Model.
    → Today: self-authorship is the field's lingua franca for developmental outcomes; critics from CRT and queer theory have charged that the telos is culturally specific, and that critique remains live but unincorporated.

CHAIN 2: The constructivist-developmental backbone (Kegan's parallel track that fused with Chain 1)
  Piaget → Kegan (*The Evolving Self* 1982, *In Over Our Heads* 1994) [not in corpus]
    → Harkins 1994: review-mediated absorption of Kegan into the higher ed conversation.
    → King & Baxter Magolda 1996; Baxter Magolda 2001: Kegan's orders of consciousness become the spine of self-authorship.
    → Abes, Jones & McEwen 2007: Kegan-via-Baxter-Magolda becomes the "meaning-making filter" in identity work.
    → Komives et al. 2006: Kegan informs leadership identity stages.
    → Today: Kegan is everywhere in student affairs theory, often more as background grammar than as cited source.

CHAIN 3: The identity-development chain that converges on epistemological development
  Erikson → Chickering 1969 / Chickering & Reisser 1993
    → Cross 1971/1991 and Helms 1990 [not in corpus]: race-specific identity models.
    → Jones & McEwen 1996: multiple-dimensions move.
    → Abes, Jones & McEwen 2007: fusion with self-authorship — this is where Chains 1 and 3 merge.
    → Torres, Jones & Renn 2009: synthesis essay that institutionalizes the merger.
    → Today: identity work and epistemological work are now hard to separate at the theoretical level, though they remain separate in practice and assessment.

CHAIN 4: The critical/cultural counter-current
  Bourdieu + Ladson-Billings (CRT in education)
    → Yosso 2005: community cultural wealth.
    → Museus & Jayakumar 2012; Museus 2017: institutional applications via CECE.
    → Today: runs alongside the developmental tradition rather than reshaping it. The deep question — whether developmental hierarchies themselves are an artifact of cultural dominance — has been raised but not absorbed into the self-authorship program.

TENSIONS THAT REMAIN LIVE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

— Is the developmental telos culturally universal or culturally specific? Self-authorship scholars (Baxter Magolda, King) treat the internal voice as a generalizable human capacity; critical scholars (Abes in her later work, scholars working from CRT and indigenous frameworks) argue that the priority placed on an autonomous internal voice over relational and communal knowing is a Western liberal artifact. Unresolved because the empirical longitudinal evidence is largely from samples where the telos is plausible, and the critique has not produced an alternative long
